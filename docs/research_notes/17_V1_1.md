## 1. Easy-benchmark B0 risk (A3) — KEEP (b) "prior centre G/2, learned width"

**Selection criterion (stated before choosing).** The criterion is lexicographic:
1. The real-data ALC must not regress in R2 or R1: point estimate ≤ 0, and the pair CI must not exclude a positive effect.
2. Among the candidates that pass, choose the one with minimum worst-case regret over the A3 sweep.
3. Mean regret breaks ties.

**Candidates (step1 and step1b).**

| candidate | R2 Δ vs v1 | R1 Δ vs v1 | max regret R2 / R1 | mean regret R2 / R1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 t3(G, sB) | — | — | 12.6 / 9.6 | 3.63 / 2.85 |
| (a) t1 (Cauchy) | +0.34 | +0.49 [+0.20,+0.76] | 10.0 / 8.2 | 2.90 / 2.52 |
| (a) mix 0.2·t3(0,2.5) | +0.26 | +0.39 [+0.08,+0.70] | 8.6 / 7.1 | 2.90 / 2.46 |

| **(b) centre G/2, learned width** | **−0.11** | **−0.35** | **7.7 / 6.2** | 3.57 / 2.75 |

The two step1b rows use a candidate set different from step1, so their regrets are not comparable with the rows above.

**C1 (BS).**
- **CLAIM:** Centring the offset prior halfway between the training mean and logit 0, with its width re-estimated around that centre, halves the easy-benchmark loss. The δ = +3 loss vs v0 falls from +12.4 to +5.7 in R2 and from +6.8 to +1.7 in R1, with no real-data cost.
- **MECHANISM:** With 4–5 training benchmarks, G is a noisy estimate of the hidden-benchmark mean. Shrinking it toward the symmetric point p = 0.5 is a James–Stein-style hedge. Re-learning the width (s′ = rms(means − G/2) = 0.78, vs sB = 0.81) keeps the observed spread covered.
- **PRIOR:** Red team A3. Matharena (an easy benchmark) loses at every few-pair regime.
- **EVIDENCE:** The tables above. It is the only candidate that improves both criteria with a real-data point estimate ≤ 0 in R2 and R1.
- **COUNTEREVIDENCE:**
  - The per-fold pattern is matharena −7.7, while the other three folds are +1.7 to +3.7 (R2). This is a trade between benchmarks, not a free gain. The benchmark-level CI is wide: h[−5.2, +3.3].
  - Hard benchmarks lose part of v1's advantage: at δ = −3, v1_1 is −4.4 vs v0 where v1 was −10.6.
- **COMPETING EXPLANATION:** Any shrink toward 0.5 does the same, and κ = 0 is close. But κ only acts at B0, while (b) also helps B1 and B3.
- **FALSIFICATION TEST:** On the next formative or summative feedback, v1_1 B0 should be no worse than v1 B0 on the benchmarks whose observed base rate exceeds 0.5. Formative feedback is never used to fit parameters.
- **DECISION:** ADVANCE.
- **CONFIDENCE:** moderately supported for the minimax improvement; weak for any mean ALC gain.

**Disagreement (CO vs MM).**
- CO prefers (b) + mix 0.2: lowest worst case (4.6 / 3.5) at a real-data cost of +0.27 / +0.18 that is not significant.
- MM vetoes: one extra mixture parameter for a worst-case gain that exists only under the synthetic δ ≥ +2 regime, with a positive point cost on real data.
- **Resolution:** rule 1 of the stated criterion is applied, so (b) alone ships.

## 2. False-merge guard (A5) — KEEP "momd", ρ0 = 0.05

The model compares two hypotheses per item key: a shared difficulty β, or independent items. It uses exact marginal likelihoods, L_same = ∫∏p(y|c_j+β)N(β) against L_ind = ∏q_j^y(1−q_j)^{1−y}, and the prediction is P(shared|y)·item + (1 − P)·q.

Four variants of the prior merge rate ρ were tried:

| variant | real R1 | real R3 | merge m=5 R1 / R3 (v1−L2: +4.9 / +4.9) | m=20 R1 / R3 (v1−L2: +12.7 / +15.8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed ρ = 0.1 per key | +0.02 | +0.13 [+0.02,+0.23] | −1.2 / −2.5 | −2.6 / −4.5 |
| EM-pooled ρ_b | +0.02 | +0.05 | −1.6 / −4.3 | −3.5 / −6.9 |
| moment ρ_b (within/cross collision) | **+0.27 [+0.15,+0.43]** | **+1.69** | −3.6 / −6.2 | −8.7 / −11.5 |
| **momd** (disagreement-weighted moment) | **+0.03 [+0.01,+0.08] h[−0.04,+0.14]** | +0.04 [−0.02,+0.09] | **−3.1 / −5.1** | **−7.3 / −10.1** |

All values are Δ vs the step-1 base. Base−L2 on real keys is −2.56 in R1 and −18.4 in R3. With momd the merge rows are m=5: +1.8 / −0.2, and m=20: +5.4 / +5.7.

**C2 (BS + SA).**
- **MECHANISM:**
  - Two labels from the *same subject* on one key must be distinct items. Distinct items appear only through a merge, or through a genuine duplicate dict.
  - The plain moment estimator flagged matharena's genuine duplicates as merges. One key there carries 14 item_ids, and 19 keys collide. This caused the +1.69 R3 regression.
  - Genuine duplicates agree; false merges disagree at the rate 2q(1−q). So the within-subject count is replaced by Σ s(n−s)/(2q(1−q)).
  - ρ_b = (n_w · P_x/P_w + k0ρ0)/(n_x + k0), with floor ρ0 = 0.05 and k0 = 2.
- **COUNTEREVIDENCE:** At m = 20, harm vs L2 remains (+5.4 / +5.7). The guard removes about 60% of it, not all. All merge evidence is synthetic.
- **DECISION:** ADVANCE as insurance. The real-data cost is 0.03e-3; the hier CI includes 0.
- **MM:** accepts. The guard has one parameter, about 30 lines, and an exact fallback to v1 when no key collides.

## 4. Acquisition (Phase 3) — DROP; keep accept-all

The simulator (`simulate_policy`) replays the streaming protocol:
- fixed random order;
- permanent skips;
- nested caps, with pairs advanced in sorted order within each checkpoint;
- forced acceptance when items_remaining ≤ labels_remaining.

Every policy reached 31 labels. A1 reproduces the engine exactly (diff 0).

Policies:
- A2: accept if |p − ½| ≤ |q − ½|.
- A3: Fisher information about c ≥ that of an unseen item.
- A5: difficulty-tercile balance among items with evidence.
- A8: exact one-step expected Brier reduction, Var_y of the updated pair mean, ≥ that of an unseen item.

**Factorial (Δ ALC vs A1 under the same predictor; R1, 100 draws).**

| policy | skip rate | v1 | v1_1 | v1_1 B1 / B31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A2 uncertainty | 14% | −0.26 [−0.46,−0.07] | −0.27 h[−0.58,−0.03] | **+0.13** / −1.36 |
| A3 Fisher | 5% | −0.11 [−0.19,−0.05] | −0.13 h[−0.27,−0.03] | 0.00 / −0.52 |
| A5 stratification | 12% | −0.11 | −0.13 h[−0.34,+0.00] | 0.00 / −1.02 |
| A8 expected-Brier | 4% | −0.11 [−0.17,−0.05] | −0.11 h[−0.22,−0.02] | 0.00 / −0.43 |

- The predictor effect (v1_1 − v1) is −0.32 under every policy, so there is no interaction.
- **R2 (2 seeds):** all |Δ| ≤ 0.04.

**C4 (AL).**
- **CLAIM:** Under v1/v1_1, acquisition cannot beat random by a useful margin.
- **MECHANISM:**
  - The predictor is item-agnostic unless a candidate's key already has labels from another subject.
  - In R2 every candidate therefore has the same prediction and the same information. Any policy that depends on the candidate only through (prediction, labeled, context) selects a uniformly random subset; it is equivalent to accept-all. The residual ≤ 0.04 comes from the self-pair's own duplicate dicts.
  - In R1 only the 4–14% of candidates with cross-subject evidence can be told apart.
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - The gains (−0.1 to −0.3) are beyond pair noise, and the hier CI excludes 0 for A2, A3 and A8.
  - They are 7–20× below the 2e-3 threshold and concentrate at B31.
  - A2 shows a B1 regression (+0.13, not significant). This is the high-budget-only pattern of §12.
- **COST:** A hook that computes the posterior runs in the parent on a deep copy of `labeled` for every candidate. It adds a fail-the-submission surface ("a malformed hook or failed call fails the submission").
- **DECISION:** REMOVE (gate F/K). Re-open only when a content-based item-difficulty prior exists (the Jev distillation); that is the only route to candidate-level information in R2.
- **CONFIDENCE:** established for R2 (by construction); strongly supported for R1.

## 5. Runtime cache — DROP (not needed)

Latency of the packaged v1_1 per call:

| case | latency |
|---|---|
| 12 subjects × 31 labels (formative scale), 2k-char content | 3.3 ms |
| same, 78k-char content | 3.7 ms |
| 300 × 34 labels | 106 ms |
| 2000 × 5 labels | 222 ms |

In red-team A7 (v1_1), latency is 89–298 ms at 10k labels, against 78–666 ms for v1.

Why the cache is not needed:
- **Bound on labels.** Under the documented ~1000-source-item draw limit, |labeled| ≤ 31·⌊1000/80⌋ = 372. That is about 4 ms per call, with A + 6E ≤ about 6k calls, so seconds in total against an 8 h limit.
- **The platform copy dominates.** The platform copies `labeled` for every call, at 5.6–230 ms measured by a JSON round trip. That costs as much as or more than `predict` at every scale. A correct cache must key on a full content fingerprint, about 10% of the parse cost, and could at most halve wall time.
- **State.** A cache would add cross-call state, against the README's "must not rely on earlier calls".
- **Hot loop.** The parse loop was tightened instead (`_str` fast path for str), with no state.

## Recommendation

**v1_1 should replace v1 for the next upload, on risk grounds rather than mean grounds.**
- The expected ALC is unchanged within noise: −0.1 (R2) and −0.3 (R1), with benchmark CIs that include 0.
- The worst case over hidden base rates halves: +12.4 → +5.7 vs v0 in R2.
- It gains insurance against false item merges, at no measurable real-data cost.

Residual risks:
- A small B31 cost (+0.1 to +0.4 Brier).
- Hard hidden benchmarks gain less than under v1.
- m ≥ 20 merges remain partly harmful.
